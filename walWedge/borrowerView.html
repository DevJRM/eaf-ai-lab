3Why the price works in your favour

Your curve charges more for later years, and this loan reaches them

A bond yield or spread tells you what investors charge to lend to you until a given date. To see what your curve implies for this particular loan, we price each six-month period of the loan separately, at the level your curve implies for that period, and then average across the life of the loan using the balance outstanding in each period.

Two features of your curve and of the loan explain the result.

A quoted level is an average

The same average life does not mean the same timing

A 12.5-year bond and this loan have the same average life, but they place their money differently in time. The bond's principal is outstanding, on average, at year 6.25. The loan's is outstanding, on average, at year 8.26, and 24% of its principal-years fall after year 12.5. On a rising curve, later exposure costs more.

4The net benefit

What you give up early, and what you gain later

Up to the average-life date, the price you pay is above what your curve implies for those years. After it, the price is well below. The second effect is larger, because the later years are where your curve is steepest. The benefit scales with the slope of your curve. If your curve steepens before signing, the benefit grows; if it flattens, the benefit shrinks. Because the price is set at signing, the benefit measured on the signing-date curve is the one you secure.

7Methodology

How the figures are derived

The analysis uses a standard reduced-form credit valuation, the same approach used to value credit default swaps and to build issuer curves. Your bond curve is the only source of credit information.

Market inputs
  • Your bond Z-spread curve, 1 to 28 years, quoted against the EUR swap curve.
  • Your bond yield curve, 1 to 28 years, annual compounding.
  • EUR swap curve against 6-month EURIBOR, 1 to 30 years, and the 6-month EURIBOR fixing of 3.019%.
  • Recovery rate of 40%, the market convention for senior unsecured debt.
  • Tenors without a quote are interpolated linearly between the nearest quoted tenors.
Steps
  1. Build risk-free discount factors from the swap curve.
  2. Derive, year by year, the default intensity that reprices each of your bonds exactly to its quoted level. This turns each quoted level, which is an average to maturity, into the cost of each individual year.
  3. Build the loan's half-yearly balance from its drawdown and repayment schedule.
  4. Price each six-month period at the cost your curve implies for it, weighted by the balance outstanding, the discount factor and the probability of no default. The weighted average is the bond-equivalent price of the whole profile.
  5. Compare it with the price read at the loan's average life. The difference, multiplied by the loan's credit-adjusted annuity of years, gives the present value of the benefit.
Points to note
  • Your curve trades below swaps at maturities up to three years. The analysis uses it as quoted, which treats the short end as cheaper than swaps.
  • Your bond yields are annual. The loan pays semi-annually, so the loan rate is the semi-annual rate with the same yield as your annual bond yield: 4.0965% annual is 4.0554% semi-annual.
  • Each quoted level is treated as the price of a bond trading at par for that maturity. For bonds far from par, a fitted issuer curve gives a cleaner input.
  • Bond issuance costs, new-issue premium and loan fees are excluded from the headline figures.
