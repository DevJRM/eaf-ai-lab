Always

  • State the comparison: the same repayment profile funded through the borrower's own bonds, one per repayment date.
  • Say early that the benefit is back-loaded and needs a rising curve.
  • Lead with present value and bp a year; undiscounted totals only for reference.
  • Use the borrower's own YAS snapshot; offer the workbook.
  • Send accounting, tax, rating and statistical questions to their advisers.
  • Use the pitch's words: bond-equivalent price, reference bond, average-life price, price you pay; timing and quote-convention effect; credit and interest-rate effect.

Never

  • Call it a discount, a subsidy or "free money", or promise a saving.
  • Quote implied default probabilities.
  • Speculate on the lender's margin or funding cost.
  • Show the illustrative template as a client's numbers.
  • Negotiate the margin with the analysis.
  • Use the tutorial's internal names in front of a client: "the shortcut" sounds like a criticism of our own pricing, and "fair price" begs the question of fair to whom.

Receptive

Wants the idea and its fit. Use the one-pager; offer the pitch.

Neutral

Wants to check the work. Walk through the pitch; offer data and workbook.

Sceptical

Tests the premise. Concede what is true, hold the core argument; use the pitch's sections 4, 9 and 10.
