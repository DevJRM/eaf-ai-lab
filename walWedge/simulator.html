Case
Sample case. Simulated market data shaped on a euro-area sovereign; not market data and not an indication of terms.

Inputs and data

The same inputs as the workbook. Market data come from one Bloomberg YAS snapshot: your borrower's Z-spreads and yields by maturity, and the swap curve YAS measures the Z-spreads against. Everything recalculates as you type; the result shows in the bar at the top.

1 · The loan
Tenor, profile and price

Drawdown and repayment in half-years, within 30 years in total. The balloon is repaid with the last instalment; the rest amortises in equal half-yearly instalments. Margin X is what is agreed above (+) or below (−) the average-life price.

Custom schedule
2 · The case
Borrower, currency and conventions

Currency chooses the interest-rate scenarios; borrower type and region choose the credit scenarios. Recovery: 40% is the market convention for senior unsecured debt; the result is insensitive to it. The coupon frequency is that of the borrower's bonds in this currency.

3 · Curves
The borrower's quotes and the swap curve

One row per fixed-coupon bond of the borrower (leave out floaters, callables and inflation-linked bonds). Maturity as a date (15/06/2033, 2033-06-15 or 15 Jun 2033, counted from "Market data as of" in card 2) or in years; coupon in %, or par for a bond trading close to 100. Paste a block from Excel or a Bloomberg export: a header row with Maturity, Cpn, Z-spread, YTM is recognised. Each bond is moved to the par bond of the same maturity, then read at whole years in the table below .

Paste a block straight from Excel or a Bloomberg export: Tenor, Z-spread, YTM and swap columns, or start in any cell. The last two columns check that the Z-spreads and yields belong to one snapshot: the yield implied by each Z-spread should match the quoted yield within 1bp .

How the model choices work

What if the curve at signing looked different?

Each scenario applies moves of the swap curve and of your borrower's credit spread, measured on official data, to today's curves; the loan is then priced as if it were signed on the result. Ordinary moves show what a normal 3, 6 or 12 months did over the last ten years; episodes replay dated market events at their most severe. They show which market conditions help or hurt the benefit: history, not forecasts. The ? marks explain where each number comes from.

Swap curve

How much swap rates move: in proportion to today's rate, plus a move by maturity. In between, the move is interpolated.

Credit spread of the borrower

How much the borrower's spread over swaps moves: in proportion to today's spread, plus a move by maturity.

Each move can be set between −100 and +100bp, in 1bp steps. "Start from" copies an episode's moves as applied to your borrower, capped at ±100bp, so you can soften or sharpen a real episode rather than start from nothing. Moving any slider switches the curve at signing to Your own scenario. The proportional sliders apply to positive rates and spreads only; the credit one runs from −60% to +200%, so its left half is finer than its right.

For a borrower the library covers only through a broad index or not at all (a specific South-East Asian sovereign, a European telecom, a US energy company), or for your borrower's own history: pull the issuer's curve on two dates from Bloomberg (for example YAS or the historical Z-spread curve) and paste it here. The moves between the two dates become the scenario.

The loan is a strip of bullet bonds, one per repayment. Each dot is that mini-bond's own price on the borrower's curve; the line is the one price the loan charges all of them. The bond-equivalent price is the average of the dots, weighted by the dot size (size × how long and how safely each is outstanding).
